# Roasted Butternut & Warm Lentil Bowl

*Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 35 min*

Last night I did not feel like making dinner. I had a mild headache and felt tired, but I still wanted a deeply nourishing, healthy meal — it needed to be effortless, quick, and easy.

This one was inspired by Woolies' Butternut & Beetroot salad — but I'd say slightly more elevated and packed with goodness (and you get more than five pieces of rocket, if you know, you know).

This was so easy, and wow, the tastes were exceptional, and it felt hearty and nourishing AF.

You can buy your butternut already diced to make it even easier, and use a tin of lentils to cut the effort further. But in all honesty, it was super quick to freshly prepare my own lentils — while they were cooking, the butternut was caramelising away in the oven.

## Ingredients

**Roasted butternut**
- 1 medium butternut, peeled and cubed (~600g)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Salt, to taste
- Black pepper, to taste
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp dried thyme or rosemary

**Warm lentils**
- 1 x 400g tin lentils (or 2 cups cooked lentils)
- 1 heaped spoonful tomato pasta sauce
- Black pepper, to taste
- 1 tsp Italian herbs

**Salad**
- 2 large handfuls rocket
- 50g feta, crumbled
- 2 tbsp toasted pumpkin or sunflower seeds (if you have them — soaking and roasting your own is easy and worth it, see [how, why, and for how long](./how-to-soak-roast-store-seeds.md))

**Balsamic dressing**
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1½ tbsp balsamic vinegar
- 1 tsp maple syrup or honey
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- Salt and pepper, to taste

**Great add-ons**
- Hemp seeds
- A squirt of lemon juice
- Flax seed
- Nutritional yeast
- Toasted pecans or walnuts (3–5 min in a dry pan — makes this taste like something you'd order at a café)

---

## Method

**1. Roast the butternut**
Toss the cubes with olive oil, salt, pepper, smoked paprika, garlic powder, and thyme or rosemary. Roast at 200°C for 30–40 minutes, turning once, until caramelised at the edges.

**2. Warm / cook the lentils**
While the butternut roasts, warm the lentils in a small pot with the tomato sauce, black pepper, and Italian herbs.

**3. Make the dressing**
Whisk together the olive oil, balsamic vinegar, maple syrup or honey, Dijon, salt, and pepper — or do it properly and grab one of the many glass jars collecting beneath the kitchen sink, tip everything in, lid on, and shake it up.

**4. Toss the salad**
Mix the roasted butternut with the feta, rocket, and toasted seeds, then add the dressing and toss to coat.

**5. Assemble**
Spoon the warm lentils into the base of your bowl, then spoon the dressed butternut salad on top.

The warm butternut softens the rocket slightly, the feta goes creamy, and the balsamic ties it all together.

## Ayurvedic Adjustments

This bowl is naturally well-balanced — warm cooked elements, raw greens, healthy fats, and, notably, all six Ayurvedic tastes in one dish: sweet (butternut, maple syrup/honey), sour (balsamic, feta), salty (salt, feta), bitter (rocket), pungent (black pepper, garlic powder, smoked paprika, Dijon), and astringent (lentils, seeds). Covering all six in a single meal is considered a marker of a satisfying, balancing meal in Ayurveda. Here's how to lean it toward each dosha.

**Vata** (dry, cold, irritable when out of balance — needs warm, moist, grounding, oily)
This recipe already suits Vata well; roasted root veg and an oil-rich dressing are grounding. To tighten the fit: swap the olive oil for ghee when roasting the butternut, add a pinch of cumin or fresh ginger to the lentils for extra warmth, and lightly wilt the rocket in the warm lentils rather than serving it fully raw and cold. Keep the balsamic dressing, and finish the assembled bowl with a fresh drizzle of lemon juice — sour is grounding and digestive for Vata. Go a little heavier on the seeds too, for extra oiliness.

**Pitta** (fiery, sharp, intense when out of balance — needs cooling, less oil and spice)
Vinegar as a category is heating (ushna virya) and, being sour, aggravates Pitta on its own — balsamic included. But balsamic is milder than most: made from reduced grape must rather than a sharp distilled acid, it carries a natural sweetness alongside the sour, and sweet pacifies Pitta. That sweetness is reinforced here by the maple syrup or honey in the dressing, and the vinegar quantity is small relative to the whole bowl, so keep it as is. The real heating culprits are the smoked paprika, black pepper, and garlic powder — ease off or skip these. Add cooling mint or coriander to the salad, keep the rocket (bitter greens suit Pitta well), lean toward maple syrup over honey, and go lighter on the feta.

**Kapha** (heavy, slow, cold when out of balance — needs light, dry, warm, spicy)
Cut the oil in both the roasting and the dressing by about half, and skip the feta or use a very light sprinkle. Lentils are already excellent for Kapha (light, astringent) — lean into them. Turn up the pungent, heating spices: extra black pepper, a pinch of chilli, or ground mustard seed. Use raw honey instead of maple syrup, go heavier on the rocket, and keep the seeds and nuts to a small portion since they're rich in fat.
